In the excluding-fixed case, an event's chance of being kept therefore depends on its decade, so event rates per period in that case partly measure USGS practice, not the Earth. Do not interpret rate changes between periods as physical.

## USGS-ISC matching (window 20 s, 100 km, one-to-one)

* 536 matches. Time difference median 1.37 s, 95th percentile 4.7 s, max 9.32 s.
* Distance median 12.07 km, 95th percentile 41.08 km, max 85.43 km; 12 matches are more than 50 km apart (median distance before 2000: 13.3 km, from 2000: 11.2 km).
* Magnitude difference (USGS minus ISC-chosen) median 0.1; for matches with the same magnitude type: median 0.00, |difference| > 0.5 in 15 of 469.
* The magnitudes agree closely, which supports the matches being the same earthquakes. Matches more than 50 km apart by decade: 1970s 3, 1990s 3, 2000s 5, 2010s 1 (listed in match_diagnostics.csv; check them by hand before relying on those events).

## Possible duplicate events

* 11 pairs of rows lie within 60 s and 150 km of each other (close_pairs.csv). 7 rows are flagged as possible duplicates (rule: within 60 s and 50 km, at least one row ISC-only; column possible_duplicate_of).
* Flagged pairs: origin-time differences 0.8-39.0 s, distances 0.8-25.5 km; magnitude sources of the flagged rows: ISC:ISC 3, ISC:NDI 2, ISC:IDC 2.
* Flagged rows stay in the catalog. The analysis scripts leave them out only with --drop-duplicates (results in results/combined_usgs_isc_nodup/).
